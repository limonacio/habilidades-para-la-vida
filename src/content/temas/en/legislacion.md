## CEDAW (1979/1981)

- The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women sets out basic norms and obligations (human rights) that establish the minimum rights and freedoms governments must uphold.
- These norms are interdependent and indivisible: some rights cannot be guaranteed at the expense of others.
- Its preamble expresses concern that, in situations of poverty, women have the least access to food, health, education, training and employment; and it states that an economic order based on equity, and the elimination of racism, colonialism and foreign domination, are essential for equality.

> **Key idea**
>
> Human rights are interdependent and indivisible: some cannot be guaranteed at the expense of others.

## Convention of Belém do Pará

In the preamble, the States Parties:

- Recognize that full respect for human rights is enshrined in the American Declaration and the Universal Declaration.
- Affirm that violence against women is a violation of human rights and limits women's enjoyment and exercise of them.
- Express concern that it is an offense against human dignity and a manifestation of historically unequal power relations between women and men.
- Recall that it cuts across all sectors of society: class, race or ethnic group, income, culture, education, age or religion.
- Are convinced that eliminating it is essential for women's individual and social development and their full and equal participation.

## Law 26,485: protected rights

It guarantees the rights recognized by CEDAW, the Convention of Belém do Pará, the Convention on the Rights of the Child and Law 26,061 on the Comprehensive Protection of Children and Adolescents; in particular: a life free of violence and discrimination; health, education and personal safety; and physical, psychological, sexual, economic or property integrity.

## Art. 2 – Purpose of the law

1. To eliminate discrimination between women and men in all areas of life.
2. Women's right to live a life free of violence.
3. Adequate conditions to raise awareness, prevent, punish and eradicate discrimination and violence.
4. The development of cross-sector public policies on violence against women.
5. The removal of cultural patterns that sustain gender inequality and power relations over women.
6. Access to justice for women who suffer violence.
7. Comprehensive assistance in public and private services.

## Definition of violence and indirect violence

- Violence against women: any conduct, action or omission that, directly or indirectly, in public or private settings, and based on an unequal power relationship, affects their life, freedom, dignity, integrity and personal safety. It includes violence by the State and its agents.
- Indirect violence: any discriminatory conduct, action, omission, provision, criterion or practice that puts women at a disadvantage compared to men.

## Art. 7 – Guiding principles

- All three branches of government (national or provincial) must fully respect the constitutional right to equality.
- To eliminate discrimination and inequality in power relations.
- To raise public awareness, promoting values of equality and delegitimizing violence.
- Comprehensive, timely assistance with free, fast, transparent and effective access; and to promote the punishment and re-education of those who commit violence.
- Mainstreaming across all measures, with inter-institutional coordination and budget resources.

## Chapter II – Competent body

It must create and maintain a registry of specialized NGOs, in coordination with each jurisdiction, and sign agreements for prevention, monitoring and assistance to women who suffer violence, and for the rehabilitation of the men who commit it.

## Full reference texts

Articles quoted from the updated text of each law published on Infoleg (Argentina's official legal information system). Unofficial translation.

### Law 26,485 – Art. 2: purpose (full)

- a) The elimination of discrimination between women and men in all areas of life.
- b) Women's right to live a life free of violence.
- c) Adequate conditions to raise awareness and to prevent, punish and eradicate discrimination and violence against women in all its forms and settings.
- d) The development of inter-institutional public policies on violence against women.
- e) The removal of sociocultural patterns that promote and sustain gender inequality and power relations over women.
- f) Access to justice for women who suffer violence.
- g) Comprehensive assistance for women who suffer violence in public and private services that run programs for women and/or in specialized violence services.
- h) Women's digital rights and assets, and their development and continued presence in digital spaces (added by Law 27,736, 2023).

### Law 26,485 – Art. 3: protected rights (full)

It guarantees the rights recognized by CEDAW, the Convention of Belém do Pará, the Convention on the Rights of the Child and Law 26,061, and in particular:

- a) A life free of violence and discrimination.
- b) Health, education and personal safety.
- c) Physical, psychological, sexual, economic or property integrity.
- d) Respect for their dignity, reputation and identity, including in digital spaces (text as amended by Law 27,736).
- e) To decide about their reproductive life, the number of pregnancies and when to have them (Law 25,673).
- f) Privacy, and freedom of belief and thought.
- g) To receive adequate information and advice.
- h) To have comprehensive assistance, protection and safety measures.
- i) To have free access to justice.
- j) Real equality of rights, opportunities and treatment between men and women.
- k) Respectful treatment of women who suffer violence, avoiding any conduct, act or omission that causes revictimization.

### Law 26,485 – Art. 4: definition (current text)

"Violence against women is understood as any conduct, by action or omission, based on gender, that, directly or indirectly, in both the public and private spheres, in analog and digital spaces, and based on an unequal power relationship, affects their life, freedom, dignity, physical, psychological, sexual, economic or property integrity, political participation, as well as their personal safety. This includes violence committed by the State or its agents."

"For the purposes of this law, indirect violence is any discriminatory conduct, action, omission, provision, criterion or practice that puts women at a disadvantage compared to men."

Clarification: the definition quoted further above is the original wording. Law 27,736 (October 2023) replaced the article and added digital spaces.

### Law 26,485 – Art. 7: guiding principles (full)

The three branches of government, national or provincial, shall adopt the necessary measures and reaffirm, in each of their actions, full respect for the constitutional right to equality between women and men. They must guarantee:

- a) The elimination of discrimination and unequal power relations over women.
- b) The adoption of measures to raise public awareness, promoting values of equality and delegitimizing violence against women.
- c) Comprehensive, timely assistance for women who suffer any type of violence, ensuring free, fast, transparent and effective access to services created for that purpose, and promoting the punishment and re-education of those who commit violence.
- d) The principle of mainstreaming in all measures and in the implementation of regulations, with inter-institutional coordination and budget resources.
- e) Encouraging cooperation and participation by civil society, engaging private entities and non-state public actors.
- f) Respect for the right to confidentiality and privacy, prohibiting the reproduction for private use or public disclosure of information about situations of violence against a woman without her authorization.
- g) Guaranteeing the existence and availability of financial resources to meet the objectives of the law.
- h) All actions needed to give effect to the principles and rights recognized by the Convention of Belém do Pará.

### Law 26,485 – Art. 9: powers of the competent body (first paragraphs)

The competent body (in the text of the law, the National Women's Council) must, among other functions:

- a) Draft, implement and monitor a National Action Plan for the Prevention, Assistance and Eradication of Violence against Women.
- b) Coordinate actions with national, provincial and municipal bodies, and with universities, unions, businesses, religious organizations and civil society organizations.
- c) Convene and set up an unpaid (ad honorem) Advisory Council with civil society and academic organizations.
- d) Promote the creation of comprehensive, free assistance services in each jurisdiction.
- e) Guarantee approaches that empower women who suffer violence, without allowing models that include any form of mediation or negotiation.

The paragraph on the registry of non-governmental organizations belongs to this same article.

### Penal Code – Art. 80 (full)

"Life imprisonment shall be imposed, and the provisions of article 52 may be applied, on anyone who kills:"

1. Their ascendant, descendant, spouse, ex-spouse, or the person with whom they have or had an intimate relationship, whether or not they lived together.
2. With cruelty, treachery, poison or other insidious means.
3. For a price or a promise of payment.
4. For pleasure, greed, or hatred based on race, religion, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity or its expression.
5. By a means capable of creating a common danger.
6. With the premeditated participation of two or more people.
7. To prepare, facilitate, commit or conceal another crime, to secure its results, to ensure impunity for themselves or others, or for not having achieved the intended aim when attempting another crime.
8. A member of the public security, police or prison forces, because of their function, position or status.
9. By abusing their function or position, when they are a member of the security, police or prison forces.
10. Their military superior in front of the enemy or armed troops in formation.
11. A woman, when the act is committed by a man and involves gender-based violence (femicide).
12. With the purpose of causing suffering to a person with whom they have or had a relationship as described in paragraph 1.

Final paragraph: if, in the case of paragraph 1, there are extraordinary mitigating circumstances, the judge may impose a prison sentence of 8 to 25 years; this does not apply to anyone who previously committed acts of violence against the woman who was killed.

### Convention of Belém do Pará (approved in Argentina by Law 24,632, 1996)

- **Art. 1 (official English text):** "For the purposes of this Convention, violence against women shall be understood as any act or conduct, based on gender, which causes death or physical, sexual or psychological harm or suffering to women, whether in the public or the private sphere."
- **Art. 3 (official English text):** "Every woman has the right to be free from violence in both the public and private spheres."
- **Art. 2 (summary):** it covers physical, sexual and psychological violence in the family, in the community, and violence committed or tolerated by the State.
- **Arts. 4 and 5 (summary):** they recognize women's human rights (life, integrity, personal safety, legal protection) and the free exercise of their civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights.
- **Art. 6 (summary):** the right to be free from discrimination and to an education free of stereotyped patterns.
- **Art. 7 (summary):** States condemn violence and commit to adopting policies to prevent, punish and eradicate it.

### Law 26,743 – Gender identity (art. 2)

"Gender identity is understood as the internal and individual experience of gender as each person feels it, which may or may not correspond to the sex assigned at birth, including the personal experience of the body."
