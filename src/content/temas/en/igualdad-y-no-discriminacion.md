## Multiple and intersectional discrimination

- The Inter-American system uses the concept of "intersectionality" to analyze discrimination when several vulnerability factors come together (for example: being a girl, a woman, living in poverty and living with HIV).
- The intersection of identities and risks increases human rights violations against historically discriminated people and groups.

> **Key idea**
>
> When several vulnerable conditions intersect (being a girl, a woman, living in poverty, living with HIV), rights violations become more severe.

## State obligations

- To prevent and eradicate violence and discrimination, and to act with due diligence.
- To guarantee the effective exercise of the rights to health and work.
- To pay special attention to women living in poverty (economic rights) and to intersecting risk factors.
- Chapter 4 of the Compendium: the principle of equality and non-discrimination implies a reinforced obligation to protect groups in vulnerable situations.

## Women as a historically discriminated group

- The IACHR identifies women as a traditionally discriminated group and stresses the urgent need for States to take new actions to promote and protect equality.
- Discrimination significantly and broadly undermines the enjoyment of human rights.
- Inter-American standards rely on the American Declaration (ADRDM), the American Convention (ACHR) and the Convention of Belém do Pará.

## CEDAW: discrimination and gender equality

- **Discrimination against women:** "any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment or exercise by women" of human rights in any field.
- **It includes gender-based violence:** violence directed against a woman because she is a woman or that affects women disproportionately: physical, mental or sexual harm, threats, coercion and deprivation of liberty.
- **Gender equality:** the ability of all human beings, regardless of sex, to develop their personal abilities, pursue their careers and make choices without the limits set by stereotypes, rigid gender roles and prejudice.
- **State obligation:** an immediate, comprehensive and multisectoral policy to eliminate discrimination.

## Law 26,485 (Decree 1011/2010)

Argentina's law on comprehensive protection to prevent, punish and eradicate violence against women in all areas of their interpersonal relationships. The full text of the articles is in the [Legislation](/en/temas/legislacion/) topic.

- **Definition:** violence against women is any conduct, action or omission that, directly or indirectly, in public or private settings, and based on an unequal power relationship, affects their life, freedom, dignity, physical, psychological, sexual, economic or property integrity and their personal safety. It includes violence by the State or its agents.
- **Art. 2 – Purpose:** to eliminate discrimination between women and men; to guarantee the right to live free of violence; to create conditions to raise awareness, prevent, punish and eradicate; to develop public policies.
- **Art. 7 – Guiding principles:** all three branches of government must guarantee equality; eliminate discrimination and unequal power relations; raise public awareness; provide comprehensive, free, fast and effective assistance.
- **Chapter II – Competent body:** a registry of specialized NGOs and agreements for prevention, assistance to women and rehabilitation of men who commit violence.

## A figure

In Argentina there were 133 femicides in the first four months of 2019, according to the gender violence observatory "Ahora que sí nos ven".
