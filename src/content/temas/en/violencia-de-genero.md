## Definition (Law 26,485, art. 4)

*Unofficial translation; the legally valid text is the original in Spanish.* Violence against women is any conduct, by action or omission, based on gender, that directly or indirectly, in public or private settings, in physical or digital spaces, and based on an unequal power relationship, affects their life, freedom, dignity, physical, psychological, sexual, economic or property integrity, their political participation and their personal safety. It includes violence committed by the State or its agents.

> **Key idea**
>
> Gender-based violence is not an individual or "couple" problem: it is a social, cultural and political problem.

## Gender-based violence and intersectionality

- It is an expression of historically unequal power relations between genders: a social, cultural and political problem, not an individual, couple or family one.
- It refers to the social subordination of women and LGBTI+ people.
- Intersectionality: when gender intersects with age, social class, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, migrant status, Indigenous identity, rural life or imprisonment, vulnerability increases, and this should guide public policy.

## Types of violence (art. 5)

- **Physical:** against the body, causing pain, harm or the risk of harm.
- **Psychological:** emotional harm and lower self-esteem; it seeks to degrade or control through threats, harassment, humiliation, manipulation, isolation, surveillance, excessive jealousy, blackmail and so on.
- **Sexual:** violates the right to decide about one's sexual or reproductive life, with or without penetration; it includes rape within marriage, forced prostitution, exploitation, harassment, abuse and trafficking.
- **Economic and property:** control of resources: keeping or destroying belongings and documents, limiting the resources needed for a decent life, controlling income, lower pay for the same work.
- **Symbolic:** stereotyped patterns, messages, values or signs that reproduce domination and normalize subordination.
- **Political:** undermines or prevents political participation on equal terms.

## Forms or settings (art. 6)

- **Domestic:** committed by a family member (kinship, marriage, cohabitation, partners or dating relationships, current or past), whether or not they live together.
- **Institutional:** officials or agents of public institutions who delay, obstruct or prevent access to public policies and rights; it includes parties, unions, business and sports organizations.
- **Workplace:** discrimination in hiring, promotion or job stability (requirements about marital status, motherhood, age, appearance, pregnancy tests); unequal pay; systematic psychological harassment.
- **Against reproductive freedom:** violates the right to decide the number of pregnancies and the spacing between births (Law 25,673).
- **Obstetric:** dehumanizing treatment, over-medicalization and pathologizing of natural processes (Law 25,929).
- **Media:** spreading stereotyped messages and images that insult, discriminate, humiliate or legitimize inequality.
- **In public spaces:** sexual conduct or remarks in public places, or places open to the public, that harm dignity and create a hostile environment.
- **Public-political:** intimidation, harassment, discrediting or threats that prevent or limit political life.

## How to recognize it: the iceberg of violence

- Some forms are explicit (physical violence, sexual abuse, threats); others are less obvious (sexist humor, controlling behavior, advertising).
- The most extreme forms are the most punished; there is more tolerance for the others, which are part of the same system of values and mandates.
- The visible tip of the iceberg: murder, physical assault, rape, abuse, shouting, threats, insults.
- The invisible base: humiliation, contempt, emotional blackmail, guilt, sexist humor, being put down.

## Extreme violence: femicides, travesticides and transfemicides

- These are the attempted and violent deaths of cis women, trans women, travestis and people who suffer lethal violence because of their identity, gender expression or sexual orientation. They are the most extreme expression of patriarchy.
- Femicide: the violent death of cis women committed by men simply because they are women; it is the extreme end of a continuum of violence. It includes killings outside the home, by strangers or in contexts of sexual violence.
