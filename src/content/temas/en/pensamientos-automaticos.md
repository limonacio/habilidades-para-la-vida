## What they are

- Cognitive processes that lead to negative internal interpretations and cause unnecessary distress and suffering.
- Everyone constantly describes the world to themselves: we label every event as good or bad, frightening or pleasant, dangerous or safe.
- These labels come from a continuous inner dialogue, a cascade we rarely notice, but which can create intense emotions and lead us to hurt ourselves or the people we love.
- They are experienced like a reflex, with no prior reasoning, and taken as valid. The concept is associated with Aaron Beck's cognitive therapy; Albert Ellis worked on related ideas in rational emotive therapy.
- We talk to others differently (rationally) than we talk to ourselves (sometimes harshly or with contempt). E.g., after losing a job: "I'm a failure", "no one will hire me".

> **Key idea**
>
> Automatic thoughts are experienced like a reflex: they appear without prior reasoning and we take them as true.

## Characteristics

1. **They are specific, discrete messages:** e.g., "She doesn't love you".
2. **They seem like shorthand:** a few words or a brief image, in telegraphic style ("alone", "sick", "I can't stand it"). A short phrase stands for a whole group of memories, fears or self-reproaches.
3. **They are almost always believed:** however irrational they are, they are barely noticed, so they are not questioned or analyzed logically.
4. **They feel spontaneous:** they pop into the mind suddenly and create stereotypes or judgments that seem true.
5. **They are phrased as "should" or "have to":** every "should" brings guilt or a loss of self-esteem.
6. **They tend to catastrophize:** they predict disasters and assume the worst; they are the biggest source of anxiety.
7. **They are hard to turn off:** they come and go on their own and set off chains of related thoughts.
8. **They are learned:** from childhood, shaped by family, friends and the media.
9. **They are fairly personal:** each person responds differently to the same situation.

## Tunnel vision

Anger, anxiety or depression come from focusing on one group of automatic thoughts and excluding the opposite ones. It is selective attention: we take in some signals from the environment and discard the rest, which produces one dominant, very painful emotion.

How it is "built" (what to avoid):

- Not paying attention to automatic thoughts or analyzing them.
- When anxious: keeping your attention on danger.
- When depressed: putting yourself down and dwelling on losses.
- When angry: focusing on the injustice and wickedness of others.

## How to recognize them

General advice: listen to unpleasant thoughts and identify them by remembering what you thought right when you felt the emotion. They are like lightning: fast and hard to catch.

1. **Reconstruct the problem situation:** replay it in your imagination until the emotion comes up and watch your thoughts "in slow motion", sentence by sentence, noticing how you interpret other people's actions ("she's bored", "he's dominating me").
2. **Expand the shorthand phrase:** complete it back to the original idea. E.g., "crazy" = "I feel like I'm losing control, and that could mean I'm going crazy".

## The thought diary

You write an entry every time you notice an unpleasant feeling, including everything you tell yourself.

| Time | Emotion | Situation | Thought | % true (before / after) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9:30 | Anger | Stuck on the highway | I'm late, the boss is angry, this is the last straw. | 90% / 40% |
| 11:00 | Anxiety | Doing extra work | I'll be here all night. I can't stand it. | 70% / 30% |
| 15:30 | Anger | Given a new assignment | Why don't they hire someone if there's so much work? | 90% / 30% |

- At the end of the day, reread the diary and fill in the percentages.
- Systematic observation shows that thoughts seemed believable when they appeared, but over time they seem less true.
- It shows how they distort reality and trigger distorted emotions and perceptions.
- Use it for several weeks, whenever you notice emotions that cause distress.
- It helps to rate how true a thought is in the moment, and again once the emotional storm has passed.
