## What is sexual diversity?

It is the full range of sexual identities, orientations and expressions that exist among people. The concept recognizes that there is no single "correct" way to be: human sexuality is diverse and shows up in many ways.

> **Key idea**
>
> There is no single "correct" way to be: human sexuality is diverse and shows up in many ways.

## Sex and gender

- **Sex:** biological and physiological characteristics (chromosomes, reproductive organs, hormones, physical traits) related to anatomy and reproductive functions.
- **Gender:** a social and cultural concept: the expectations, roles, behaviors and traits that society attributes to people according to their assigned sex. It is a social construction of what it means to be a man or a woman.

## Sexual orientation

It is the emotional, romantic or sexual attraction a person feels toward others.

- **Heterosexuality:** attraction to people of the opposite sex.
- **Homosexuality:** attraction to people of the same sex.
- **Bisexuality:** attraction to people of more than one gender or sex.
- **Asexuality:** lack of sexual attraction to others.

## Gender identity

It is each person's inner, personal sense of their own gender. It may or may not match the sex assigned at birth, and it shows in how the person feels, expresses themselves and presents to society. It is not determined by biological sex.

- **Cisgender:** gender identity matches the sex assigned at birth.
- **Transgender (trans):** gender identity is different from the sex assigned at birth.
- **Non-binary:** gender identity does not fit the categories of man or woman.

## Why LGBTI+ rights matter

- Everyone has the right to be treated with respect, to feel proud of who they are and to love whoever they love.
- Many LGBTI+ children and teenagers are still thrown out of their homes, and LGBTI+ people cannot walk the streets freely and safely.
- Discrimination based on sexual orientation or gender identity violates many human rights: education, comprehensive health care, employment, access to justice and participation in public life.
- According to the UN, the State must actively work to protect these rights.

## History and laws in Argentina

- **1946:** Electoral Law 5,109 of the province of Buenos Aires, signed into law by governor Domingo Mercante, excluded homosexual people from voting "on grounds of unworthiness". That ban was only repealed in 1990, by Law 11,019.
- **1951:** an amendment to the Code of Military Justice banned homosexual people from joining the Army.
- **2003:** the City of Buenos Aires and the province of Río Negro created civil unions regardless of sex.
- **2006:** the collective agreement for the national public administration (Decree 214/2006) bans workplace discrimination based on sexual orientation.
- **2010:** Equal Marriage Law 26,618: the same marital rights for all couples, including adoption and recognition of same-sex parent families.
- **2012:** Gender Identity Law 26,743: trans people can register the name and sex they identify with on their documents.
- **2012:** Law 26,791 amends art. 80, paragraph 4 of the Penal Code, punishing hate crimes based on sexual orientation, gender identity or its expression with life imprisonment.
- **2015:** the new Civil and Commercial Code gives same-sex and different-sex couples equal access to legal forms of union.
- **2015 and 2019:** the province of Buenos Aires passes Law 14,783, known as the Diana Sacayán Law (2015), which sets a job quota of at least 1% in the provincial public administration for travesti, transsexual and transgender people. It was put into effect in 2019.

## Today: violence and hate crimes

- Surveys show high rates of psychological and emotional violence (especially in relationships) and discrimination at school and at work.
- National Observatory of LGBT+ Hate Crimes: 102 hate crimes between January 1 and June 30, 2025, a 70% increase over the same period of the previous year.
- Trans women are the most affected: 86% of the attacks on the right to life.
- Even so, most of society holds an inclusive view: hate attacks are rejected and condemned by the media.
