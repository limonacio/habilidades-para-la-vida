## It is neither "natural" nor "irrational"

Violence in football (soccer) is not limited to the **barras bravas** (organized groups of hardcore fans). It also includes everyday practices in the sport that are normalized as part of its "folklore."

- **Nobody sees themselves as violent:** violence always seems to be something "others" do. Pointing to some forms of violence hides others.
- **It is not senseless:** people who commit violent acts are often called "beasts," "crazy" or "savages." But violence is the result of social and cultural causes, and it has meanings for those who use it.
- **It is not a matter of poverty:** fan groups bring together people from working-class and middle-class backgrounds (Alabarces, 2004; Garriga Zucal, 2007).

> **Key idea**
>
> Violence is learned and reproduced culturally, within a patriarchal and sexist model: that is why it can be prevented.

## "Aguante"

Among fan groups, **aguante** (literally, "endurance") has different meanings. It can mean cheering nonstop in the stands, or it can mean fighting (Garriga Zucal, 2007).

- In its violent version, belonging to the group requires fighting: "enduring" blows, tear gas and repression is proof of courage and manhood.
- Violent acts set up hierarchies and give honor and prestige; not fighting is experienced as a dishonor, as a "lack of manhood."
- That identity is unstable: it is not enough to say you have aguante, you have to prove it again and again.

This connects with a model of masculinity that links being a man with strength and endurance of pain (see [Patriarchy and masculinities](/en/temas/patriarcado-y-masculinidades/)).

## Violence legitimized beyond the barra

Many spectators who are not part of the organized fan group also commit violent acts. In football, violence enjoys broad acceptance: people who reject it in other contexts accept it at the stadium. It is enabled, for example, when:

- some journalists confuse it with folklore;
- officials present members of the barras as an example of "passion";
- club directors welcome the barras as ambassadors of the club.

## What can be done

- **Work on what legitimizes it:** the idea that it is "natural" and part of everyday life.
- **Work with all the actors involved:** officials, journalists, players, families and young people, and especially with the media.
- **Professionalize the security forces**, so that they intervene without making the violence worse.
- **Don't label anyone as "violent":** that label presents people as unable to change. If violence is seen as part of their nature, repression is the only option left; if it is understood as something learned, there is room for prevention.
- **Build rules for living together** in clubs and recreational spaces, based on respect and the rejection of violence.
