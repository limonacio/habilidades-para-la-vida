## Where the concept comes from

- In the 1980s, Maurice Godelier and Pierre Bourdieu studied the Baruya (Oceania) and the Berbers (Africa) to understand everyday male domination.
- Their conclusions help us understand the usual, hidden ways in which men place themselves "above" women in relationships.
- Most men have not fully abandoned the codes of domination and exclusion of women passed down from generation to generation.
- Other authors (Miller, Bourdieu, Glick, Castañeda) called these behaviors petty tyrannies, intimate terrorism, "soft" violence, invisible machismo or benevolent sexism. In the 1990s, Luis Bonino named them "micromachismos" (micro-machismos, mM).

> **Key idea**
>
> Micro-machismos are subtle, everyday behaviors whose main aim is to hold back women's change and keep the relationship unequal.

## Types of power

- **Power of domination:** the ability to control other people's lives or actions; it is imposed and exercised openly or covertly.
- **Self-affirming power:** the ability to act and transform; the personal strength to exist, decide and assert oneself with autonomy.
- **Other-affirming power:** different from self-affirming power; in our culture it is legitimized for women and not for men.

## What micro-machismos are

They are "small", everyday uses of the power of domination: subtle, repeated and almost invisible behaviors of "very low intensity" that let men do what they want and prevent women from doing the same. Their aim is less to subjugate than to resist women's change.

## The four categories

- **Utilitarian:** they force women's availability by taking advantage of the domestic and caregiving aspects of the traditional role.
- **Covert:** they hide their aim of imposing one's own reasons by abusing women's trust and credibility.
- **Crisis:** they force the unequal status quo to remain when it becomes unbalanced (because the woman gains power or the man loses power of domination).
- **Coercive:** they keep power through men's psychological or moral force.

## Utilitarian micro-machismos

- They are strategies of overloading the woman by avoiding responsibilities: they work through what is left undone and delegated to her, so she loses vital energy for herself.
- They are the most normalized and invisible ones, and one of the most effective forms of male resistance to change.
- There are at least two groups; the first is not taking responsibility for domestic tasks.

## Covert micro-machismos

They are insidious and subtle, and that is why they are so effective: the aim of forcing the woman's availability is hidden behind "other reasons", and they lead her to hold back her own wishes.

1. **Creating a lack of intimacy:** the man controls the rules of the relationship through distance. Forms: (a) silence: not feeling obliged to talk or explain, imposing a lack of dialogue; (b) isolation and manipulative bad mood: putting physical or mental distance when she seeks connection; (c) withholding recognition and availability ("ninguneo", ignoring her): not acknowledging her contributions, which creates a hunger for affection and makes the little he gives seem very valuable.
2. **Pseudo-negotiation:** distorted communication: the problem is discussed to keep up appearances, with no real willingness to move from one's own position.
3. **Playing innocent:** believing one doesn't have to account for one's actions. (a) Blaming innocence: judging the woman for not fulfilling her "traditional role" ("you're exaggerating", "you're crazy"); the man is always the judge and never responsible. (b) Self-indulgence and self-justification: excuses for not doing shared tasks, blocking fair complaints.

## Micro-machismos and change

- To different degrees, they undermine freedom, autonomy and the ability to choose.
- Men should recognize and change them; women should know them and their effects in order to resist and expose them.
- Making them visible encourages change in women and greater accountability in men.
- Reducing micro-machismos leads to more equal and healthier relationships for everyone.
