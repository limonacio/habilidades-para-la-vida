## Deep (diaphragmatic) breathing

- Place one hand on your chest and the other on your abdomen; breathe in deeply, bringing the air down to your belly.
- A helpful image: filling a canteen, from the bottom up. The hand on your abdomen rises; the one on your chest barely moves.
- Each breath stretches and relaxes the abdomen and sends your brain the message that everything is okay.

> **Key idea**
>
> Each deep breath sends your brain the message that everything is okay.

## Breathing with stressful scenes

1. Choose and write down two scenes that cause moderate anger (between 5 and 7 on a scale of 1 to 10).
2. Divide each scene into three parts, with a reminder to breathe at the end of each one.
3. Visualize the first scene part by part, breathing with your diaphragm at the end of each part; repeat two or three times.
4. Visualize the second scene once without deep breathing and note your stress level; then repeat it at least twice with deep breathing and notice how you feel.

## Relaxation without tension

So far, progressive relaxation has been practiced by first increasing tension; now that step is dropped, using a relaxation technique without tension.

## Cue-word relaxation

- Choose a personal word or phrase ("relax", "let go", "okay"), a color or a feeling that brings you well-being.
- With diaphragmatic breathing, say it or think it with each exhale while you let your whole body loosen.
- Repeat ten times in a row until the response becomes automatic.
- In stressful moments: go back to the earlier scenes and replace deep breathing with the cue word.
- Practice both techniques every day for the following weeks.

## Facing a real stressful situation

Once you've learned the elements, practice with an everyday situation: go over the scene without relaxing to find which part of your body holds the most tension, and then focus the relaxation on those muscle groups.

## Chi Kung: guided practice

**Chi Kung** (or *Qigong*) is a practice of Chinese origin that combines slow movements, breathing and attention. **Shi Ba Shi** is a series of 18 simple exercises that can be done standing up and with no previous experience. It is a good way to practice the diaphragmatic breathing and relaxation on this page while moving.

Follow the series in order, at your own pace, without forcing any movement. If you have an injury or a health problem, check with a professional first.
