## What it is

In 1979, American psychologist Lenore Walker described how, in many violent relationships, the violence is not constant: it follows a **cycle that repeats**. Popular versions present it in three or four phases; here we use the four-phase version.

> **Key idea**
>
> The calm periods and the promises to change are part of the same cycle: they don't mean the violence is over.

1. **Honeymoon:** a romantic period in which everything seems fine.
2. **Tension building:** friction grows, along with the feeling that an explosive argument could happen at any moment.
3. **Violent episode:** violence breaks out, in any of its forms: physical, psychological, sexual, economic or symbolic (see [Gender-based violence](/en/temas/violencia-de-genero/)).
4. **Fear of losing the relationship:** regret and promises to change appear (gifts, money, making up), and things return to the honeymoon.

## Why it matters

- It helps explain why many people stay in a violent relationship or go back to it: the calm periods and the promises feed the hope that everything will change.
- It helps recognize the pattern: it isn't one isolated "fight", but a dynamic that keeps repeating.
- Recognizing it is a first step toward asking for help and supporting others without judging them.
