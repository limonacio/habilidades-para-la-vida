## Paranoid personality

They attribute hidden motives to others and distrust them, suspecting that information will be used to harm them. They live with unbearable levels of shame and humiliation and avoid guilt by blaming others. According to Colby and Faught (1981), paranoia is a set of strategies to prevent or reduce shame, built on an underlying belief of being inadequate and insufficient.

- **Self:** sees themselves as righteous and mistreated.
- **Others:** devious, dishonest, treacherous and manipulative.
- **Beliefs:** "I'm vulnerable", "you can't trust other people", "they have bad intentions".
- **Threat:** being put down, manipulated, devalued or discriminated against.
- **Strategy:** constant vigilance, caution and suspicion.
- **Affect:** anger at the supposed abuse and constant anxiety about perceived threats (often the reason they seek therapy).

> **Key idea**
>
> Each personality style is organized around beliefs about oneself and others, and a strategy to protect against what one fears most.

## Obsessive-compulsive personality

It is defined by "control" and "duty"; order becomes a virtue. Often raised in a rigid, controlling family, they have no close relationships, work in a technical, detail-oriented job and have no leisure activities.

- **Self:** responsible for themselves and others; believes it is up to them that things get done right.
- **Others:** careless, irresponsible, self-indulgent or incompetent.
- **Beliefs:** "I could be overwhelmed", "I need order and a system of rules", "any mistake will make me fall apart".
- **Threat:** flaws, errors, disorganization and imperfections; losing control is a catastrophe.
- **Strategy:** a system of rules, norms and duties used to judge their own behavior and that of others.
- **Affect:** remorse, disappointment and self-punishment; anxiety about falling short and depression after a serious failure.

## Narcissistic personality

The key is "self-aggrandizement".

- **Self:** special and unique; deserves special treatment and is above the rules.
- **Others:** inferior; they should pay tribute.
- **Beliefs:** "I deserve the best", "others have to recognize my worth".
- **Threat:** anything that could hurt their self-esteem.
- **Strategy:** manipulation to reinforce their status and expand their personal power.
- **Affect:** anger when they don't get the admiration they believe they deserve; depression when their strategies fail.

## Schizoid disorder

- **Self:** self-sufficient and solitary; values independence and making decisions alone.
- **Others:** intrusive and controlling.
- **Beliefs:** "I'm alone", "close relationships aren't worth it", "I do better when others don't get in my way".
- **Threat:** too much closeness.
- **Strategy:** keeping their distance.
- **Affect:** low-level sadness while keeping their distance; strong anxiety if forced into close contact. They seem not to have intense feelings.

## Dependent disorder

They see themselves as helpless and need to attach to someone stronger who will provide what they need to survive and be happy.

- **Self:** needy, weak, helpless and incompetent.
- **Others:** an idealized strong caregiver who nurtures and supports them.
- **Beliefs:** "I need someone stronger"; happiness depends on a constant flow of support.
- **Threat:** rejection or abandonment.
- **Strategy:** cultivating the relationship by being submissive and trying to please or appease.
- **Affect:** anxiety about a possible breakup; depression if they lose that person; euphoria when their dependency needs are secured.

## Avoidant personality

They would like to be close to others and fulfill their potential, but fear being hurt, rejected or failing.

- **Self:** socially inept and incompetent at school or work.
- **Others:** potentially critical, uninterested or demeaning.
- **Beliefs:** "I'm not good enough", "I'm unworthy", "I can't stand unpleasant feelings" (suppressed at any cost: alcohol, drugs).
- **Threat:** being exposed as a fraud, humiliated or a failure.
- **Strategy:** avoiding situations where they might be judged, social gatherings and drawing attention.
- **Affect:** dysphoria (anxiety and sadness); low tolerance for frustration; very introspective and sensitive to their own distress.

## Passive-aggressive disorder

An oppositional style that hides a wish for recognition from authority figures. Its central conflict: wanting the benefits authority offers while also keeping autonomy.

- **Self:** passive and submissive to keep the relationship, but rebels when feeling a loss of autonomy; sees themselves as self-sufficient yet vulnerable to abuse.
- **Others:** authority is intrusive, demanding and controlling, but also able to approve and care.
- **Beliefs:** "being controlled is intolerable", "I have to do things my way", "I deserve approval".
