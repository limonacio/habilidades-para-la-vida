## The family changes

For the psychiatrist Jorge Barudy, the family is an **open system**, in constant interaction with society: what happens inside reflects what happens outside.

For a long time a single model of the "ideal" family was imposed: the married heterosexual couple, with the man as provider and the woman in charge of the home and child-rearing. But the way of living as a family has changed throughout history, and today many forms coexist:

- **Nuclear:** a couple, with or without children.
- **Single-parent:** a mother or a father with their children, for example after a separation or a death. When the mother raises the children alone from the start, that family is sometimes called **single-mother** (in Spanish, *monomarental*).
- **Extended:** includes grandparents, uncles and aunts or other relatives, and also emotional bonds without blood ties.
- **With separated parents:** the parents do not live together, but both keep caring for and raising the children.
- **Blended:** one of the adults forms a new couple and there is at least one child from a previous relationship.
- **Adoptive:** welcomes a child through adoption.
- **Diverse:** formed by people of the same gender, with or without children. In Argentina, Equal Marriage Law 26,618 (2010) grants them the same rights as heterosexual couples.

> **Key idea**
>
> There is no single right way to be a family: what matters is good treatment and care.

## The family is not always a refuge

We tend to think of the family as a place of love, incompatible with aggression. But, as Cecilia Grosman and Silvia Mesterman point out, it is sometimes a **school of violence**, where children learn that aggression is a way to control others.

Outside pressures (work, money, unresolved problems) can strain family life, but they do not explain or justify violence: when that tension is unloaded onto women or children, it is because there is an unequal power relationship (see [Gender-based violence](/en/temas/violencia-de-genero/)). That is why, beyond covering basic needs, it is necessary to promote **good treatment**, and for the State to support families in vulnerable situations (not only economic ones).

## When the couple separates

Before the absolute divorce law (Law 23,515, 1987), it was believed that parents' separation turned children into sick people or criminals. Today we know that a separation is painful and can leave marks, but **painful is not the same as damaging to health**.

- Young children often ask themselves "if they love me, why are they separating?", and sometimes believe it is their fault. It helps to tell them clearly that it is not.
- What does the most harm is not the separation itself, but **ongoing conflict between the adults**.

What to avoid:

- **Undermining the other parent:** phrases like "don't listen to your mother" or "your father doesn't know what he's talking about" confuse children about whom to respect.
- **Making them take sides** or form alliances to "beat" the other parent.
- **Asking them to keep secrets:** "don't tell Dad about this" puts them in a place that is not theirs.
- **Blurring roles:** treating them as "friends" or confidants in the adults' conflict, instead of protecting them.

These guidelines are for separations without violence. When there is violence, protecting the children is not "undermining" the other parent or "turning them against" them (see [Effects of gender-based violence on children](/en/temas/efectos-en-las-infancias/)).

Beyond the separation, both parents remain responsible for raising and protecting their children. This is what the Argentine Civil and Commercial Code calls **parental responsibility**.
