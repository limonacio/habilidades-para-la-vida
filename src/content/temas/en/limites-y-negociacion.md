## Learning to set boundaries

Being able to say "no" is essential: it helps others understand our needs and priorities. People who can't set boundaries may end up feeling anger.

Three basic guidelines:

1. **Acknowledge the other person's needs:** ask for more information with direct questions and, if you don't understand the request, rephrase and repeat it to check that you heard it correctly.
2. **State your own position:** give the reason for the boundary, including your feelings and preferences, without apologizing, with confidence and assertiveness. E.g., "I need to rest this weekend."
3. **Say "no":** this is the essence of a boundary: "no, thank you", "I don't want to do that", "I've decided not to go".

> **Key idea**
>
> In a negotiation, the other person's needs are as important as your own: the goal is that nobody loses.

## Tips for saying "no"

- **Don't apologize:** apologizing signals that you don't feel entitled to take care of yourself, and invites the other person to push harder.
- **Don't put yourself down:** phrases like "I can't because I'm weak/clumsy" are not a boundary: the other person will try to convince you that you can. Better: "I won't do it" or "I don't want to do it", which leave less room for argument.
- **Watch your voice and body language:** make eye contact when you set the boundary.
- **Delay your answer:** if you tend to say "yes" automatically, buy time: "I'll let you know later." Without pressure, you think more clearly.
- **Be specific:** make clear what you are willing to do and what you aren't. E.g., "I'm willing to review your project, but I won't take part in producing it."

## Negotiation as a way to reduce problems

- It is a method that helps reduce conflicts of interest.
- It means listening to the other person without trying to prove them wrong.
- It starts from the premise that the other person's needs are as important as your own.

## The six steps of negotiation

1. **Know what you want:** be as specific as possible; vague statements like "I want to be happy" don't help.
2. **Express it in behavioral terms:** say clearly what you expect the other person to do. E.g., "I'd like you to do the dishes before you watch TV."
3. **Listen to the other person's point of view:** gather information instead of arguing, using active listening (open questions, clarifications, paraphrasing). Understanding doesn't mean agreeing.
4. **Make a proposal:** consider what the other person needs and what they gain; it takes creativity and flexibility.
5. **Ask for a counterproposal:** if they don't like your proposal, encourage them to suggest an alternative that works for both sides.
6. **Reach a compromise:** this is the heart of negotiation. E.g., "If we do it my way this time, next time we'll do it yours."

Closing thought: in negotiation nobody loses; everybody wins.
