## The ABC of emotional problems

- **A – Activating event:** it seems to cause the emotional reaction, but it is not the cause.
- **B – Belief system:** this is what really causes the consequence.
- **C – Consequence:** the emotional reaction.
- **D:** where the solution to the problem is found.

> **Key idea**
>
> It is not the event (A) that causes the emotion (C), but what we believe about it (B).

## Irrational myths

Widespread ideas in Western culture that lead to problematic thoughts and negative emotions:

1. **"Everyone must love us and approve of what we do":** it is impossible to please everyone; trying makes us more insecure and less independent.
2. **"We must be competent and achieve everything we set out to do":** no one does everything well; perfectionism creates anxiety and fear of failure, and keeps us from enjoying our achievements.
3. **"There are wicked people who hurt us on purpose and must be punished":** few things are absolutely good or bad; an inappropriate behavior doesn't make someone a bad person.
4. **"If things aren't the way I want, it's a catastrophe; the world should be fair":** the universe doesn't revolve around us; better to say "what a pity" and do something constructive.
5. **"Unhappiness comes from outside events we can't control":** most of it comes from what we tell ourselves about those events; we can control our inner responses.
6. **"We should worry about dangerous things until they pass":** worrying doesn't stop them from happening; most of what we fear never happens or is less serious.
7. **"It's easier to avoid difficulties than to face them":** avoiding them creates more problems; facing them builds confidence and self-esteem.
8. **"We need someone stronger to lean on":** some dependence is normal, but our life doesn't depend on someone else; excessive dependence makes both people insecure.
9. **"The past determines present behavior":** changing what we learned is hard but not impossible; we should learn from the past without clinging to it.
10. **"What others do is vitally important, and we must change them":** other people's behavior is their business; trying to change it makes things worse. Live and let live.
11. **"Every problem has a perfect solution":** there are many possible solutions and rarely a perfect one; choose a good one and, if it doesn't work, try another.
12. **"We have no control over our emotions":** we can learn to manage them instead of letting them manage us.
13. **"We have the right to a life without pain":** adversity is part of nature; improve what you can and accept what can't be changed.
14. **"I know what's best for other people":** individuality is valuable; each adult should take care of their own affairs.

## The three categories of irrational thinking (Ellis)

1. About oneself: "I must do well and win approval, or I'm incompetent and unworthy of love."
2. About others: "You must treat me kindly and fairly, or you're despicable."
3. About life: "Conditions must be favorable, or life is awful and unbearable."
