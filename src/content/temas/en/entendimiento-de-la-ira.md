## The "benefits" of anger and their cost

- **It reduces stress:** it temporarily relieves worry, frustration, unmet needs or pain; but the discomfort comes back if it isn't worked on.
- **It hides emotional pain:** it is a defense against fear, loss, guilt, shame and rejection. It blocks feelings that could signal what we need to do or stop doing.
- **It gets other people's attention:** shouting makes others try to calm us down, but over time they become immune, get defensive and resentment grows.
- **It works as punishment and revenge:** it brings brief relief, but turns the people we love and need most into enemies.
- **It helps change others:** in dysfunctional families people learn to use anger to pressure others; in the long run, others pull away.

> **Key idea**
>
> The way we express anger is learned; that is why we can also learn healthier ways to express it.

## Myths about anger

1. **"Anger is hereditary":** false. Expressing anger is a learned behavior (by watching parents, family and friends), so more appropriate ways can be learned too.
2. **"Anger automatically leads to aggression":** false. Managing anger well means precisely controlling its escalation.
3. **"You have to be aggressive to get what you want":** this confuses aggression with assertiveness. Aggression seeks to dominate, intimidate or hurt; assertiveness expresses anger respectfully, without blaming or threatening.
4. **"It is always good to let your anger out":** people used to believe that shouting or hitting a pillow was therapeutic; research found otherwise.

## The anger meter

A technique to become aware of anger: 1 is complete calm; 10 is an explosive loss of control with negative consequences; the numbers in between mark the levels between both extremes.

## The aggression cycle

1. **Escalation:** signs appear that anger is starting: physical, behavioral, emotional or cognitive.
2. **Explosion:** an uncontrolled discharge of physical or verbal aggression; it equals 10 on the meter.
3. **Post-explosion:** negative consequences: jail, paying damages, losing a job, being expelled from a treatment program, losing loved ones, guilt, shame and regret.

## How we get angry

1. **An experience of pain:** physical or emotional (stomachache, fatigue, rejection, loss) that frustrates needs or threatens our safety.
2. **Trigger thoughts:** interpretations and assumptions that make us feel like victims of deliberate harm; they blame and condemn others.

## When anger becomes a problem

- When it is felt too intensely or expressed in inappropriate ways.
- Physical effects: blood pressure and heart rate stay high for long periods; risk of hypertension, heart disease and a weaker immune system.
- Consequences: violence or physical aggression, arrest, injuries, retaliation, losing loved ones.
